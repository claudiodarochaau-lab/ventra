A record year for international students, and the number that still lags

Insights Published 25 September 2026

Claudio Da Rocha

Founder and CEO, Ventra

TL;DR

  • Record year: international students rated their overall educational experience at all-time highs in 2025: 76.8% for undergraduates and 77.5% for postgraduates. The share who considered leaving fell to a record low of about 12%.
  • Belonging still lags: it also hit an all-time high, but at 59.1% for undergraduates it sits well below every other measure (74–85%). Roughly four in ten international undergraduates don't rate their sense of belonging positively.
  • Being on campus isn't enough: international students are the most on-campus group, yet belonging still trails. Financial difficulty (30.7%) and stress (30.6%) are the top reasons they consider leaving.
  • What institutions can do: belonging is built in the ordinary week. That means reasons to stay on campus, places to gather, and daily basics students can afford.

The 2025 Student Experience Survey International Report gives the sector plenty to be pleased about. International students rated their overall educational experience at record highs: 76.8% positive for undergraduates and 77.5% for postgraduate coursework students. All five areas the survey measures reached record positive ratings. The share of international students who considered leaving their institution fell to a record low of about 12%.

One number deserves more attention than it will get.

Belonging is improving, but it's still the weakest result

Sense of belonging also reached an all-time high: 59.1% for international undergraduates and 62.2% for postgraduates. But even at its best, it sits well below everything else. For international undergraduates, the other areas range from 74.2% (Peer Engagement) to 85.1% (Skills Development). Roughly four in ten international undergraduates don't rate their sense of belonging to their institution positively.

The National Report explains why that matters. Belonging has historically been rated low compared with the rest of the student experience. It also tracks closely with how students rate their overall experience, their support services and their teaching.

Being on campus isn't the same as belonging

International students are the most on-campus group in higher education, because their visa conditions require it. Being there does help. Nationally, undergraduates who attend all their classes on campus rate belonging at 54.4%, against 40.5% for those studying entirely online.

But it isn't enough on its own. International students are on campus more than anyone else, and four in ten still don't feel part of it. The gap between being there and belonging there is where institutions have room to act.

There's another clue in the data. International undergraduates at non-university providers rate belonging higher than those at universities: 63.0% against 57.4%. The report doesn't say why. A reasonable guess is that smaller, more contained campuses make it easier for students to find each other.

Why students think about leaving

Among international undergraduates who considered leaving, the most common reasons were financial difficulties (30.7%), stress (30.6%) and mental health (30.4%). Another 17.5% cited a lack of connection to their institution.

For these students, cost and connection are closely linked. When money is tight, a student who can't afford to spend time on campus between classes goes home. The time between classes is exactly when students meet the people who make the place feel like theirs.

What institutions can actually influence

Belonging isn't built at orientation. It's built in the ordinary routine of a semester: having a reason to stay on campus between classes, somewhere to be with other people that isn't a classroom, and daily basics students can afford on a part-time wage.

That's the part of campus Ventra works on. At Charles Sturt University's Sydney and Melbourne campuses, delivered by Navitas, students get specialty-grade coffee priced well below typical café rates, and nobody on campus has to manage it.

We won't claim coffee fixes belonging. It doesn't, and this data couldn't show that it does. But it is one of the small daily reasons to stay on campus. It's also one of the few an institution can put in place in weeks rather than years.
