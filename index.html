The problem

Campus coffee fails in complexity and accountability.

A machine from one vendor. Beans from another. Service from a third, on their schedule. When something breaks, it becomes your team's job to notice, report, and chase — while the person standing at the machine is a student deciding whether this campus has its act together, or a staff member deciding whether the place they work for pays attention to the details.

Fragmented supply

Multiple suppliers, multiple contracts

Equipment, beans, milk, consumables and maintenance — all arriving on separate agreements, separate invoices, and separate accountabilities.

Downtime

Nobody is watching the machine

A machine that stops on Monday morning stays down until someone notices, reports it, and a technician is found. Coffee is judged daily.

Admin burden

Coffee becomes someone's job

Ordering beans, logging faults, chasing callouts: operational load landing on people who were hired to support students.

The managed model

One partner managing the whole solution.

Scope and design

We survey your site, model your daily volumes, and size the equipment to real demand, then put the full commercial model in front of you before anything is signed.

Install and commission

Delivery, plumbing and filtration, commissioning, and dial-in with our roasting partner. Your team signs off on a working service, not a delivered box.

Managed from day one

Telemetry watches every machine. Beans arrive before they run out. Maintenance and service happen on our initiative, not your reminder.
